live-music-translator is a small companion app that sits on top of your music player and shows you the lyrics of whatever song is playing, translated line by line into the language you are learning. It stays floating above other windows, so you can keep your eyes on the words while you listen, sing along, or study. Everything happens on your own computer using a local AI model called Gemma, which means no accounts, no subscriptions, and no waiting for a website to load. If you have ever wished you could understand a song in Spanish, Japanese, French, Korean, or any other language the moment you hear it, this app was made for you.

## ✨ What This App Does

live-music-translator listens to the music coming out of your computer and turns it into something you can actually study from. Instead of dumping a wall of text on you, it works line by line, showing the original lyric next to its translation at the same pace as the song. That pacing is the whole point: it mimics how you would read subtitles in a film, which is one of the most natural ways for your brain to pick up new vocabulary and sentence rhythm.

Here is what you get:

- **An always-on-top window.** The lyric panel floats above your browser, your music player, your video app, or anything else you have open. It never gets buried behind other windows.
- **Line-by-line translations.** Each lyric line is translated as it is sung, so you can follow the meaning in real time instead of pausing to copy and paste text somewhere else.
- **Original and translated text side by side.** Seeing both versions together helps you connect words you already know with words that are new to you.
- **Fully offline translation.** The language model runs on your own machine. Your listening habits and your study sessions stay private.
- **Adjustable window size and text size.** Make the lyrics as large or as small as you need, and place the window wherever it is comfortable on your screen.
- **A clean, distraction-free look.** No ads, no pop-ups, no suggested videos, no notifications pulling you away from the song.

## 🚀 Getting Started on Windows

Once the app is open for the first time, you will be guided through a few friendly choices.

1. **Pick your source language.** This is the language the song is being sung in. If you are not sure, there is an automatic option that will try to detect it for you.
2. **Pick your target language.** This is the language you want the translation to appear in — usually your native language, or the language you are actively learning.
3. **Choose your lyrics window style.** You can select a light or dark panel, and you can choose how many lines appear at once. Two to four lines is a comfortable starting point.
4. **Press Start.** The app will begin listening and the first translated lines will appear in the floating window, usually within a few seconds of the song starting.

After the first setup, your choices are remembered. The next time you open the app, it starts right where you left off.

## 🎧 How to Use It While You Listen

The best way to use live-music-translator is to treat it like a subtitle track for your ears.

Start a song in whatever app you normally use — a streaming service, a local music player, or a video. The floating lyric window will follow along. Read the translated line first to catch the meaning, then glance back at the original to see how the words were arranged. If a particular line catches your attention, note it down. Repeat listens of the same song are genuinely useful; the second and third time through, you will start recognising words before the translation appears.

Keep the window small during a first listen so it feels like background support, and make it larger when you are actively studying a song you love.

## 🔒 Privacy and Offline Use

Everything the app does happens on your computer. The translation model lives on your machine, and the audio is processed locally. Nothing is uploaded, nothing is stored on a server, and nothing is shared. You can use the app with your Wi-Fi turned off entirely. This also means it keeps working on a plane, on a train, or anywhere else your connection is unreliable.

## 💡 Tips for Getting the Best Results

- **Start with songs you already know.** Familiar melodies make it easier to focus on the words rather than the tune.
- **Use headphones when you can.** A cleaner audio signal means more accurate line timing.
- **Keep other audio quiet.** Turn off notifications, muted videos, and background calls during a study session so the app is only hearing the music.
- **Choose shorter songs at first.** Three to four minutes is a perfect practice length before your attention drifts.
- **Write down three new words per song.** A small, repeatable habit beats a long list you never revisit.

## ❓ Troubleshooting

**The lyric window does not appear.** Check the taskbar and your system tray. The app may be running behind another window, or it may be minimised. Clicking the desktop shortcut again will bring it to the front.

**No lyrics are showing up during a song.** Make sure the music is actually playing through your computer's speakers or headphones and that the volume is not muted. The app needs to hear the audio to follow along.

**The translations feel slow.** The first song after launching can take a little longer while the local model wakes up. Subsequent songs are noticeably quicker.

**The window is too small or too large.** Drag any edge of the floating window to resize it, or use the text size control inside the app settings.

**Windows shows a security warning during setup.** This is standard for independent software. Click "More info," then "Run anyway," and the setup will continue normally.
